SUPERLIMINAL

The design philosophy behind Liminal Labyrinth is rooted in a overwhelming admiration for SuperLiminal, a game that redefined how we perceive space and perspective. Every element here, from the tone of the voiceover, to the surreal narrative beats and visual aesthetic, is a direct homage to its clever puzzle design and dreamlike atmosphere.

Liminal Labyrinth was built to capture just a fraction of that perspective-shifting magic. If traversing this maze resonated with you, stepping into the world of Superliminal is the natural next step. It is a masterpiece in every sense of the word, and an absolute must-play.

MIRROR'S EDGE

I’ve always believed that music is the true heartbeat of any world worth exploring. Whether it's the rich score of a film or the ambient backdrop of a video game, an underlying symphony has the power to strike precise chords within your mind. It transforms a simple journey into something profound, bridging the gap between playing a game and truly living inside its environment.

Mirror’s Edge, released all the way back in 2008, achieved this magic effortlessly. As you fluidly leap across its stark, pristine architecture, the soundtrack delivers a euphoric, dreamlike harmony that feels completely inseparable from the world itself. It’s an iconic auditory experience—one that still gives me goosebumps to this day.

ILLUSION MAZES

The core mechanics of Liminal Labyrinth stem from the illusion maze genre, drawing immense inspiration from julius (make sure to check out his Illusion Maze on Habbo Origins!). His rooms may appear deceptively simple at first glance, but hours later you'll find yourself trapped in the exact same loop, searching for a way out with his stunning visual design making the journey an absolute pleasure.

Alongside these spatial illusions, the Labyrinth often operates as a paradoxical puzzle. Your brain instinctively overcomplicates the rules, assuming invisible walls and complex barriers stand between you and the exit. In reality, once you abandon those initial assumptions, the path forward becomes delightfully trivial. If you’ve ever braved markeh’s A Horrible Maze, this spatial trickery might feel strangely familiar! His deception maze was a major spark of inspiration when Liminal Labyrinth was first conceived.

BACKROOMS

The concept of the Backrooms has always been deeply intriguing to me, the unsettling thought of a hidden world coexisting within our own reality, yet completely detached and impossibly out of reach. It's a space defined by infinite yellow corridors, flickering fluorescent hums, and an overwhelming sense of isolation.

That distinct feeling of uncanny familiarity and subtle dread naturally bled into the DNA of Liminal Labyrinth. The Labyrinth wasn't just built as a set of rules to solve, but as an eerie, transitional space to inhabit; a place where every turn feels like a wrong turn, and the environment itself seems to react to your presence.

Voiceover script for room 'Approximation' takes direct quotes from the game Superliminal, written by Will O'Neill. The original full quote comes from the games closing script, and reads as follows:

Hello! My name is Dr. Glenn Pierce, and by now you may have realized that all of this has happened exactly the way it was supposed to. You see, everyone who comes to the Institute does so because they feel that they are no longer in control of something important to them. But, more often than not, the problem is not that the problems we face can't be solved. The problem is that we become so afraid of failure that we refuse to see our problems from a new perspective. And so we do the same things again and again and again. And therein, of course, we find exactly the failure we were looking for. Your life will always be a struggle and you will always have problems. But today, you had the chance to see things differently. Even though it meant facing obstacles that seemed impossible at first, you thought outside the box - and you overcame them. Because you saw things from every angle, you understood them for what they really were. Because you kept moving forward, no matter how far off the path you were told you were headed, or how unexpected it became... you found your way. In a few minutes, you'll be back in the real world, and some part of you will say that none of this was real; so how could it have really meant anything? But, just like the power of perspective itself - it will have been as real as you believed it to be. All you've got to do is wake up.
