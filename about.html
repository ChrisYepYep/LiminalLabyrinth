SUPERLIMINAL

The design philosophy behind Liminal Labyrinth is rooted in a overwhelming admiration for SuperLiminal, a game that redefined how we perceive space and perspective. Every element here, from the tone of the voiceover, to the surreal narrative beats and visual aesthetic, is a direct homage to its clever puzzle design and dreamlike atmosphere.

Liminal Labyrinth was built to capture just a fraction of that perspective-shifting magic. If traversing this maze resonated with you, stepping into the world of Superliminal is the natural next step. It is a masterpiece in every sense of the word, and an absolute must-play.

MIRROR'S EDGE

The core mechanics of Liminal Labyrinth stem from the illusion maze genre, drawing immense inspiration from julius (make sure to check out his Illusion Maze on Habbo Origins!). His rooms may appear deceptively simple at first glance, but hours later you'll find yourself trapped in the exact same loop, searching for a way out—with his stunning visual design making the journey an absolute pleasure.

Alongside these spatial illusions, Liminal Labyrinth often operates as a paradoxical puzzle. Your brain instinctively overcomplicates the rules, assuming invisible walls and complex barriers stand between you and the exit. In reality, once you abandon those initial assumptions, the path forward becomes delightfully trivial. If you’ve ever braved markeh’s A Horrible Maze, this spatial trickery might feel strangely familiar! His deception maze was a major spark of inspiration when Liminal Labyrinth was first conceived.

ILLUSION MAZES

The mechanics of Liminal Labyrinth has roots within the illusion maze category, which takes a lot of inspiration from julius (Check out his Illusion Maze in Habbo Origins!), his maze rooms can appear simple upon first glance, but you'll find yourself in the same room hours later still trying to find a route through; julius' stunning designs make his maze such a pleasure to try.

Alongside illusion, the Labyrinth can be somewhat a paradoxical puzzle at times, your brain overcomplicates the rules, and you assume there are complex barriers between you and the exit, but in actual fact once you abandon your initial assumptions the simplistic action required is trivial.
